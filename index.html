Last updated: 22 November 2026 · Version 2.0

Your privacy matters to us. This policy explains what data the "Dr Strong" app collects, how it's used, who it's shared with, and how you can control it. By using the app, you agree to the practices described below.

2. What We Do Not Collect

  • Your location. The app does not request or use location, and the iOS location permission is not declared at all.
  • No advertising. There are no ads and no ad networks in the app.
  • No tracking or behavioural analytics, and no advertising identifiers.
  • What your chat messages say. They are locked on your phone before they leave it, and we cannot read them — unless you report one (section 5).

3. How We Use Your Data

  • To display your stats and progress in the app (charts, goals, history).
  • To securely store your data so it's available whenever you sign in, on any device.
  • To estimate calories and macronutrients from food photos or barcodes.
  • To enable social features (steps challenge, step groups) when you choose to participate.
  • To compare you anonymously with other users: once a day, everyone's steps, fluids, calories and fasting are ranked together, and you see your own rank and the averages.
  • To run Friends when you use it: following, your groups and what each sees, stories and chats.
  • To tell you of a new message, through Apple's notification service.
  • To act on reports: hiding and checking a reported story, reading a reported message, and stopping an account from posting stories or sending messages.

5. What Other Users Can See

Nothing by default, beyond the anonymous comparison below. Nothing else is shared unless you join a step group or a challenge, or use Friends, yourself. When you join a group, only the members of that group can see your name, your Walker ID and your step figures: today, yesterday, this week, this month, and your streak, with the day they are for and when you joined. If you ask to join a group, its founder sees your name and Walker ID with the request. In a steps challenge, only its participants can see your name, your Walker ID and your step total for the challenge's days.

A group's name, and the account ID of whoever founded it, can be read by anyone who has its invite code, so they can see what they are joining.

Your profile photo is not shared in groups and challenges. Members see a single letter from your name in its place.

In Friends:

  • Your Walker ID lets anyone ask to follow you, and you decide. Until you accept, they see your Walker ID alone. One account can send thirty requests a day at most.
  • Your card — your name and a small copy of your profile photo — is seen by the people you follow or ask to follow, and by the followers you accepted. Who follows you, and who you follow, are seen by you alone, and so are your groups: nobody is told which group they are in.
  • Your data is seen by nobody until you choose. For each group, you choose for each of steps, water, fasting, calories, weight, body measurements, vitamin results and points whether it sees nothing, a summary, or the details. A summary is a few figures: for calories, your streak alone; for weight, the change since your first weigh-in, never the weight itself. The details are your entries themselves — every day's steps, water, fasting hours, calories and macronutrients with your meals by name, your weight, every body measurement, and every lab result with its range — with your goals for those and the day you began using the app: their app receives the whole history of each, and shows it by the period they pick. Only the followers you accepted, in that group, see it, and blocking someone hides it from them.
  • Stories are seen by the followers you accepted, in the groups you choose, for 24 hours. You see who has seen yours, and others see that you have seen theirs.
  • Chats are only between two people who follow each other. "Seen" tells the sender you have opened the chat. A message is gone 48 hours after it is sent, but the other person can take a screenshot, and nothing tells you if they do. Deleting a message of yours deletes it for both of you.

Reports are read by us alone. When you report or block someone, nobody else sees the report — not the person reported, and not the rest of the group, challenge or chat. If someone reports you, the report holds your account ID and your name as it appeared there, and you are not told.

A story that three people report is hidden from everyone, and checked: a photo by the model in section 4 — or by us, if the model gives no answer — and a video by us. If it does not break the rules it comes back; if it does, it is deleted. A message you report is sent to us by your phone as you saw it — its text, photo or voice — so that we can read it. We may stop an account from posting stories or sending messages.

The comparison is anonymous. Once a day your steps, fluids, calories and fasting are ranked among everyone's. Other users see their own rank and averages, rounded and taken across ten people or more — never your figures, your rank or your name.

Your weight, calories, body measurements and lab readings are never shown in a step group or a challenge; in Friends, only to the groups you choose (above). Your calories count in the comparison's anonymous averages alone. You can leave a group at any time, and your data disappears from it.

6. Data Security

  • Sign-in and passwords are managed via Firebase Authentication; passwords are never stored as plain text.
  • Your personal data is tied to your account only and cannot be accessed by other users, except what you choose to share with them (section 5).
  • The app talks to our server only over an encrypted connection (HTTPS), and the server reads or changes your data only for a request that carries your sign-in and Firebase App Check's confirmation that it comes from our app.
  • Data you choose to share (like a steps challenge or a step group) is only visible to participants who explicitly joined.
  • Chat messages are end-to-end encrypted. Your phone locks each one with a key only your phone and the other person's can make (ECDH on P-256, HKDF-SHA256, AES-256-GCM). Your private key stays in your phone's Keychain, on that phone alone — never in a backup or iCloud. Our server keeps messages locked and cannot read them; what it does see is who writes to whom and when, each message's type and size, and when it was seen.
  • What that does not do: the other person's key comes from our server, and the app has no way yet to compare it with them; and someone who took your phone's key could open the messages to and from it still on our server — 48 hours' worth at most.
  • Stories are not end-to-end encrypted, so that they can be shown to the people you choose, and checked when reported.

8. Your Rights

  • Access: You can review your data at any time within the app.
  • Correction: You can edit your profile and data directly from the Settings page.
  • Deletion: You can permanently delete your account and your personal data from inside the app — Profile → Delete account. Your data is removed from our server, and your sign-in account from Firebase. So is everything of yours in Friends: your follows either way, your groups and card, what your groups saw, your stories with who saw them, every chat message you sent or received — so it disappears from the other person's chat too — your public key and your phones' notification addresses; and the chat key on the phone you delete it from is deleted from its Keychain. If you signed in with Apple, the app also asks Apple to revoke its access to your Apple ID. Deletion is permanent and cannot be undone.
  • Data in groups you joined: The rows you published into a step group or a challenge (your name and step figures), and a request to join a group, are held in that group, not in your account, and deleting the account does not remove them. Leave the group, or withdraw the request, first if you want them gone, or email us and we will remove them for you.
  • Reports: A report keeps the account ID of whoever sent it and of whoever it is about, and it stays after either account is deleted, so that deleting an account cannot erase the reports about it — and so does a copy of a reported story or message, until its 7 days end, and a stop on posting stories or sending messages. Email us about a report you sent.
  • A copy of your data: You can export everything stored in your account from inside the app — Settings → Privacy → Export my data. You receive a single JSON file, a standard format that other apps and services can read. It does not include the rows you published into groups (above), or Friends — your Walker ID, follows, groups, stories and messages; email us for those — except messages, which we cannot read.

9. Children's Privacy

This app is not directed at children under the age of 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from this age group. If we become aware that we have collected data from a child under this age, we will delete it promptly.
